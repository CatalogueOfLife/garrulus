# Crossref and OAI-PMH can carry Garrulus

Garrulus can drop OpenAlex for harvesting with little loss, because **Crossref by ISSN already reaches most of the journals it follows**, including about two-thirds of the regional taxonomy titles checked. Discovery is harder: Crossref has carried no subject data since 2024, so the source registry needs new free signals. Two gaps remain. The first is abstracts: Elsevier and Taylor & Francis deposit none to Crossref, and Springer Nature deposits them only for open-access articles. The second is a long tail of small museum, society and Chinese journals with no Crossref DOIs. The routes to adopt first are: Crossref as the primary harvester, with its free annual public data file replacing the OpenAlex snapshot for backfill; a discovery stack built from the CC0 PKP Beacon dataset, DOAJ subject terms, Plazi's journal statistics and the references in the COL extended release; one generic OAI-PMH harvester, which covers ARPHA's 103 journals, Magnolia Press, the big OJS taxonomy journals and the national platforms (Hrčak, DergiPark, AJOL, CyberLeninka); and Europe PMC by ISSN to fill missing abstracts. RSS feeds are worth adding only as change signals and as a source of abstracts for Wiley, Springer and Frontiers. Several apparent alternatives fail the constraints: Unpaywall now runs on OpenAlex, JournalTOCs is offline, Lens and BASE are paid or restricted, and bioRxiv's bulk bucket is requester-pays. All findings are as of 2026-11-19.

## Crossref covers most journals but has gaps in abstracts and subjects

Crossref is the natural successor to OpenAlex for known journals, and it is already in Garrulus as the change detector. A sample of 14 core taxonomy and systematics journals shows how far it goes for 2025. Its abstract coverage is complete or nearly so for Magnolia, Pensoft, MDPI, MNHN and OUP. It is **zero for Elsevier and Taylor & Francis** ([Crossref API, totals](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=0&filter=from-pub-date:2025-01-01,until-pub-date:2025-12-31,type:journal-article,issn:1175-5326,issn:1179-3155,issn:1313-2989,issn:1055-7903,issn:0307-6970,issn:0022-2933,issn:1424-2818,issn:0363-6445,issn:1445-5226,issn:1399-560X,issn:0024-4082,issn:2118-9773,issn:0040-0262,issn:1314-4049&facet=issn:40,publisher-name:20); [with abstracts](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=0&filter=from-pub-date:2025-01-01,until-pub-date:2025-12-31,type:journal-article,has-abstract:true,issn:1175-5326,issn:1179-3155,issn:1313-2989,issn:1055-7903,issn:0307-6970,issn:0022-2933,issn:1424-2818,issn:0363-6445,issn:1445-5226,issn:1399-560X,issn:0024-4082,issn:2118-9773,issn:0040-0262,issn:1314-4049&facet=issn:40,publisher-name:20)).

The pattern matches the wider picture. **49.7% of current journal articles had open abstracts in Crossref at the start of 2024**, and Elsevier and T&F were the main holdouts ([Crossref I4OA 2023](https://www.crossref.org/blog/i4oa-hall-of-fame-2023-edition)). Springer Nature deposits abstracts only for OA content, and Wiley started depositing in August 2023 ([Kramer 2024](https://bmkramer.github.io/SesameOpenScience_site/thought/202411_open_abstracts)). The regional picture is better than expected. Of about 60 regional biodiversity journals checked by ISSN, roughly two-thirds had Crossref DOIs since 2024, most with abstracts. Examples include Revista de Biología Tropical (306 works, 297 with abstract), Neotropical Ichthyology (223/222), Revista Mexicana de Biodiversidad (178/178), Zoosystematica Rossica (89/89), Treubia (332/329) and the Journal of Threatened Taxa (679/645) ([Crossref facet query, Latin America](https://api.crossref.org/works?filter=from-pub-date:2024-01-01,issn:1984-4689,issn:0034-7744,issn:2215-2075,issn:0102-3306,issn:1677-941X,issn:1679-6225,issn:1982-0224,issn:0031-1049,issn:1807-0205,issn:0073-4721,issn:1678-4766,issn:1870-3453,issn:2007-8706,issn:0366-5232,issn:2357-3759,issn:1561-0837,issn:1727-9933&rows=0&facet=issn:100); [Crossref facet query, Asia and Europe](https://api.crossref.org/works?filter=from-pub-date:2024-01-01,issn:0082-6340,issn:2337-876X,issn:0974-7893&rows=0&facet=issn:100)). The first gain is therefore cheap. Garrulus should make sure every registered journal is queried under **both its print and electronic ISSN**: Revista Brasileira de Entomologia, for one, has 144 works under its e-ISSN but only 1 under its print ISSN ([Crossref facet query 2](https://api.crossref.org/works?filter=from-pub-date:2024-01-01,issn:0717-652X,issn:0016-531X,issn:0717-6643,issn:0718-8994,issn:1514-5158,issn:0326-1778,issn:0065-1737,issn:1405-4094,issn:0304-3711,issn:1659-4266,issn:2236-8906,issn:2175-7860,issn:1981-8114,issn:1676-0611,issn:2176-7793,issn:0085-5626,issn:0120-0488,issn:1021-0296,issn:0027-3910&rows=0&facet=issn:200)). The free ISSN-L table, about 17 MB and updated daily, is the cheap way to normalise those pairs ([ISSN-L table](https://www.issn.org/services/online-services/access-to-issn-l-table/)).

The operating constraints are tighter than when Garrulus's Crossref harvester was designed. Since 1 December 2025, list queries in the polite pool are capped at **3 requests per second with concurrency 3** ([Crossref rate-limit announcement](https://crossref.org/blog/announcing-changes-to-rest-api-rate-limits/)). From 21 July 2026 those limits count **per `mailto` address**, so the whole indexer shares one quota and must serialise list calls and read the `x-rate-limit-*` headers ([Crossref community](https://community.crossref.org/t/refining-rest-api-limits-for-improved-stability-and-reliability/16137)). This is still ample: about 2,000 journals in batches of 40 OR-ed ISSNs come to a few hundred list calls a day. For backfill, Crossref itself recommends the **annual public data file (208 GB, about 180 million records in 2026)** plus REST for the delta ([Crossref 2026 public data file](https://crossref.org/blog/2026-public-data-file-now-available/)). It is free, carries every abstract that publishers deposited, and maps directly onto the current `BACKFILL` design, which streams the OpenAlex snapshot filtered by source.

Two Crossref fields need care. **`has-license:true` does not mean open**: Elsevier, Wiley and OUP attach a TDM or publisher licence URL to every article, while Magnolia deposits no licence at all (0 of 3,273 works) ([Crossref API, has-license](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=0&filter=from-pub-date:2025-01-01,until-pub-date:2025-12-31,type:journal-article,has-license:true,issn:1175-5326,issn:1179-3155,issn:1313-2989,issn:1055-7903,issn:0307-6970,issn:0022-2933,issn:1424-2818,issn:0363-6445,issn:1445-5226,issn:1399-560X,issn:0024-4082,issn:2118-9773,issn:0040-0262,issn:1314-4049&facet=publisher-name:20)). The retention gate must therefore test the licence URL for CC terms, together with its `content-version` and start date. The second field is subjects. **Crossref removed its ASJC subject codes in April 2024 and plans no replacement** ([Crossref blog](https://crossref.org/blog/subject-codes-incomplete-and-unreliable-have-got-to-go)), so it cannot pick biodiversity journals. It can only confirm and enrich a list built elsewhere, through `/journals/{issn}` and `/members/{id}/works`.

## One OAI-PMH harvester opens hundreds of journals and some full text

OAI-PMH earns its place for three reasons. It reaches journals with no Crossref DOIs. It carries licences and galley links that Crossref omits. And on some OJS sites it delivers the full text itself. A single implementation of the protocol's six verbs, with `from` datestamps, sets and resumption tokens ([OAI-PMH 2.0](https://www.openarchives.org/OAI/openarchivesprotocol.html)), covers a large and varied set of endpoints. None of the verified endpoints blocked a plain client during about 30 requests.

| Endpoint | Scope | What it delivers |
|---|---|---|
| ARPHA `zookeys.pensoft.net/oai.php` | **103 journal sets** (ZooKeys, PhytoKeys, MycoKeys, BDJ, Check List, Arthropod Systematics & Phylogeny, Vertebrate Zoology, ...) | oai_dc with abstract, ORCIDs, DOI, PDF URL, CC BY licence; MODS with reference lists ([ARPHA ListSets](https://zookeys.pensoft.net/oai.php?verb=ListSets)) |
| Magnolia `mapress.com/zt/oai`, `phytotaxa.mapress.com/pt/oai` | Zootaxa, Phytotaxa, Megataxa, Bionomina, Palaeoentomology | abstract, DOI, issue metadata, PDF galley, **no `dc:rights`** ([Zootaxa ListRecords](https://www.mapress.com/zt/oai?verb=ListRecords&metadataPrefix=oai_dc&from=2026-11-01)) |
| EJT (OJS) | European Journal of Taxonomy | abstract, DOI, two galley URLs ([EJT ListRecords](https://europeanjournaloftaxonomy.eu/index.php/ejt/oai?verb=ListRecords&metadataPrefix=oai_dc&from=2026-10-01)) |
| JoTT (OJS 3.5) | Journal of Threatened Taxa | **`jats` format with CC BY licence and a 13–20 KB body per record** ([JoTT JATS](https://threatenedtaxa.org/index.php/JoTT/oai?verb=ListRecords&metadataPrefix=jats&from=2026-10-01)) |
| Hrčak `hrcak.srce.hr/oai/` | Croatian national platform | platform-wide ([Hrčak Identify](https://hrcak.srce.hr/oai/?verb=Identify)) |
| DergiPark `dergipark.org.tr/api/public/oai/` | Turkish national platform | platform-wide; datestamps unreliable ([DergiPark Identify](https://dergipark.org.tr/api/public/oai/?verb=Identify)) |
| AJOL `ajol.info/index.php/index/oai` | **2,870 sets** of African journals and sections | OJS 3.3 ([AJOL ListSets](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/index/oai?verb=ListSets)) |
| CyberLeninka `cyberleninka.ru/oai` | Russian OA journals, mostly CC BY in older sources | ([CyberLeninka Identify](https://cyberleninka.ru/oai?verb=Identify)) |
| e-periodica, Persée, SciELO México, Copernicus, Zenodo | Swiss, French heritage, Mexican, geoscience, repository | Copernicus offers a `pmc` (JATS) format ([Copernicus Identify](https://oai-pmh.copernicus.org/oai.php?verb=Identify)); Persée has 1,477 sets ([Persée ListSets](https://oai.persee.fr/oai?verb=ListSets)) |

The JoTT result is the most consequential for content. OJS journals that enable the PKP JATS plugin can serve full text over OAI, although the plugin "does not create JATS itself" and only exposes what the journal already produces ([pkp/oaiJats](https://github.com/pkp/oaiJats)). JoTT's body is escaped HTML from the HTML galley rather than structured JATS. That is still a better input for the finder than a PDF run through GROBID, because HTML keeps the italics that GROBID's TEI loses (ADR-0002). The harvester should ask for `jats` whenever `ListMetadataFormats` offers it. For CC-BY OJS journals without it, the galley URLs in `dc:relation` are the path to full text. HTML galleys should come first. PDF galleys would revive the deferred GROBID route 5 (ADR-0004), which needs per-host politeness and robots handling.

The endpoints vary in quality, so the adapter needs a few defensive modes. **MNHN's `sciencepress.mnhn.fr/oai` ignores `from`**: a request from 2026-10-01 returned all 29,356 records. It has no sets, empty creators and no DOIs, so it is usable only as a full dump with local filtering ([MNHN ListRecords](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/oai?verb=ListRecords&metadataPrefix=oai_dc&from=2026-10-01)). DergiPark and AJOL report the current day as their earliest datestamp, so they need a full re-listing per journal set and deduplication by OAI identifier and DOI. MDPI's sets are article types, not journals, which makes MDPI cheaper through Crossref, where it deposits complete abstracts and licences ([MDPI ListSets](https://oai.mdpi.com/oai/oai2.php?verb=ListSets)). Platform-wide endpoints such as DergiPark and AJOL are dominated by non-biology content. Turkish taxonomy is also spread across general university science journals ([DergiPark example](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/erzifbed/article/1547758)). Harvest these by curated set, never in bulk. Non-DOI works also break Garrulus's current identity rule, under which works are upserted by OpenAlex id or DOI. The OAI identifier plus landing URL has to become a third key.

Some important journals have neither OAI nor feeds. The Records of the Australian Museum, Zoological Studies, Raffles Bulletin of Zoology, Bonn zoological Bulletin and Acta Entomologica Musei Nationalis Pragae all fall in this group, as do BioOne, Ingenta (HTTP 403) and Brill titles ([journals.australian.museum](https://journals.australian.museum/); [Ingenta Mycotaxon](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/mtax/mt)). Several of these have Crossref DOIs (AEMNP 53–85 since 2024), so Crossref stays their route. Those without DOIs need a per-journal HTML adapter, which should come last.

## Discovery needs new free registries now that OpenAlex topics are gone

Garrulus seeds and discovers sources from OpenAlex group-by queries and labels their discipline from OpenAlex topics. No single free directory replaces that, but four sources together come close, and each adds something the others lack.

The **PKP Beacon dataset** is the best single find. It is CC0, on Harvard Dataverse, version 7 released 2026-07-18, and has **98,273 rows** with ISSN, OAI URL, set spec, country, total and 2025 record counts, a DOAJ link and responsiveness flags ([Dataverse dataset](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/datasets/:persistentId/?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/OCZNVY); [beacon.tab](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/14084919)). PKP counts **58,221 journals that published at least 5 items with OJS in 2025** ([PKP usage data](https://pkp.sfu.ca/software/ojs/usage-data/)). A crude keyword filter on journal names (zoolog*, taxonom*, entomol*, botan*, mycol*, fauna, flora, natural history, museum, plus Spanish and Portuguese forms) found **521 responsive contexts, 313 of them active in 2025**. The top countries were Indonesia (104), Brazil (32) and India (32). The filter is rough in both directions, because many taxonomy journals lack these words and "zoo" catches off-topic titles. Joining Beacon ISSNs to the existing registry attaches an OAI endpoint and set to known sources at no cost. Two cautions apply. Some hosts are stale: biotaxa.org, still listed for AEMNP and Revista Chilena de Entomología, now redirects to an Auckland security page. And zero counts are unreliable: Zootaxa shows `record_count_2025 = 0` despite thousands of articles. Every endpoint therefore needs a live `Identify` before use.

**DOAJ** contributes subject classification that Crossref dropped. Its journal records carry LCC subjects (155 journals under "Zoology"), the journal licence, APC, publisher and country ([DOAJ journals, Zoology](https://doaj.org/api/search/journals/bibjson.subject.term:Zoology?pageSize=1)). Article records add abstracts and full-text links, including for journals without DOIs. The European Journal of Taxonomy alone has 1,811 ([DOAJ articles, EJT](https://doaj.org/api/search/articles/issn:2118-9773?pageSize=1)). Because DOAJ lists only fully OA journals, **the journal licence applies to every article**, which settles the retention question for the whole journal. The API allows 2 requests per second, and the full dumps are now granted case by case ([DOAJ API](https://doaj.org/api/docs); [DOAJ data dump](https://doaj.org/docs/public-data-dump/)). DOAJ's OAI-PMH serves journals and articles, the latter with abstracts in `dc:description` ([DOAJ OAI-PMH docs](https://doaj.org/docs/oai-pmh/)). LCC terms can replace OpenAlex subfields as the "discipline" facet for OA journals. For the rest, Garrulus's own index-derived taxonomic focus is the better signal anyway.

Two taxonomy-native sources point straight at where names are published. Plazi's TreatmentBank statistics endpoint, `tb.plazi.org/GgServer/srsStats/stats`, needs no key and groups treatments by journal and year. According to the recovered researcher summary, it yields several hundred taxonomic journals, many small and regional, though the journal labels need cleaning. The **COL extended release** holds about 2 million references, according to the same summary. Many are bare citations such as "Phytotaxa 573(1): 91" that would need title-to-ISSN normalisation through Crossref's `/journals?query=`. Its `sectorKey` shows which source dataset cited the journal, which is a direct measure of relevance to COL ([ChecklistBank 3LXR](https://api.checklistbank.org/dataset/3LXR)). Both should rank candidates by how many names they produce, not merely list them.

Several other sources play smaller roles. **Wikidata** confirms the scale of the gap rather than filling it. Only 206 scientific journals with an ISSN have main subject zoology, 564 botany and 15 taxonomy, far below Garrulus's 2,000 ([Wikidata SPARQL](https://query.wikidata.org/sparql?format=json&query=SELECT%20%3Fs%20%3FsLabel%20(COUNT(DISTINCT%20%3Fj)%20AS%20%3Fc)%20WHERE%20%7B%20VALUES%20%3Fs%20%7Bwd%3AQ8269924%20wd%3AQ431%20wd%3AQ441%20wd%3AQ7150%20wd%3AQ7193%20wd%3AQ47041%7D%20%3Fj%20wdt%3AP31%20wd%3AQ5633421%3B%20wdt%3AP236%20%3Fissn%3B%20wdt%3AP921%20%3Fs.%20SERVICE%20wikibase%3Alabel%20%7B%20bd%3AserviceParam%20wikibase%3Alanguage%20%22en%22.%20%7D%20%7D%20GROUP%20BY%20%3Fs%20%3FsLabel)). It remains useful for enrichment: publisher, country and website. **Mir@bel** offers an open-licence API over more than 20,000 journals and their access points ([Mir@bel API](https://www.data.gouv.fr/dataservices/api-mir-bel-revues)). **SciELO ArticleMeta** lists journals per collection, 428 for Brazil alone ([ArticleMeta journals](https://articlemeta.scielo.org/api/v1/journal/identifiers/?collection=scl&limit=3)). Latindex records each journal's OAI address in its catalogue criteria but has no export ([Latindex methodology](https://latindex.unam.mx/latindex/documentos/pdfs/Catalogo_2.0_metodologia_v3.pdf)). ROAD's bulk and SRU services are paid ([ISSN ROAD](https://issn.org/?p=5314)). The PKP Index was retired in 2022 ([PKP](https://pkp.sfu.ca/?p=4263)). JournalTOCs, once the obvious free catalogue of feed URLs, **no longer resolves in DNS**. Its last Wayback capture is from August 2025 ([Wayback CDX](http://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=journaltocs.ac.uk&limit=-5&output=txt)).

## Europe PMC, publisher APIs and selected feeds close the abstract gap

Europe PMC is the strongest free top-up for the publishers that withhold abstracts, wherever the journal is PubMed-indexed. A 2025 query returned **2,021 Zootaxa records with abstracts** from MEDLINE ([Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=ISSN:%221175-5326%22%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:2025%20AND%20HAS_ABSTRACT:y&format=json&pageSize=2&resultType=core)). All 587 ZooKeys articles of 2025 are there as OA full text ([Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=ISSN:%221313-2989%22%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:2025%20AND%20IN_EPMC:y%20AND%20OPEN_ACCESS:y&format=json&pageSize=1&resultType=lite)). Phytotaxa is mostly absent, contributing at most about 200 of its 1,040 articles ([Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(ISSN:%221175-5326%22%20OR%20ISSN:%221179-3155%22)%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:2025&format=json&pageSize=1&resultType=lite)). The practical rule is to query Europe PMC by ISSN and index date only for journals whose Crossref abstract share is low. Whether that rescues Elsevier and T&F biodiversity titles such as Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution or the Journal of Natural History was not checked, and should be tested per journal before relying on it. Europe PMC also indexes more than 30 preprint servers, among them bioRxiv, Research Square, Preprints.org and EcoEvoRxiv ([Europe PMC preprints](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11426508/)). That makes it simpler than harvesting each server. Where a direct feed is wanted, the free bioRxiv API returns abstract, category, licence and a JATS path per preprint ([bioRxiv API](https://api.biorxiv.org/)).

Publisher APIs add a narrow but real slice. The **Springer Nature Meta API's free Basic plan returns abstracts for subscription articles**, which Crossref lacks. The Open Access API serves JATS full text at 500 calls a day ([SN Open Access API](https://datasolutions.springernature.com/products/open-access/); [SN MetaData API](https://datasolutions.springernature.com/products/meta-data/)). With a page size of 25 and an unconfirmed monthly cap, it suits a daily top-up of SN biodiversity journals, not a backfill. Elsevier, Wiley and T&F tie text and data mining to subscriptions. Their OA articles fall under the article's own licence, so CC-licensed full text can be fetched from publisher links, but there is no free API route to anything else ([Elsevier TDM policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining); [T&F TDM policy](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/textanddatamining/)).

RSS feeds are cheap change signals but rarely a primary source. They are worth polling where they carry abstracts Crossref lacks. **Wiley** feeds (`/feed/<eISSN>/most-recent`) give full abstracts, DOI and authors ([Taxon feed](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/feed/19968175/most-recent)). **Springer** search feeds give full abstracts but need a cookie jar to pass a redirect ([Fungal Diversity feed](https://link.springer.com/search.rss?facet-journal-id=13225&facet-content-type=Article)). **Frontiers** feeds give structured abstracts but use a lowercase `<pubdate>` that strict parsers miss ([Frontiers feed](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/rss)). The others add little. T&F feeds have no abstract. ScienceDirect feeds have **no DOI and no abstract**, and assert that "all rights are reserved, including those for text and data mining, AI training" ([MPE feed](https://rss.sciencedirect.com/publication/science/10557903)). MDPI's feed answers 403. Feeds also hold only the last 10–100 items, so busy journals need daily polling with ETag handling. The storage question is unresolved: no publisher was found that explicitly permits or forbids automated polling and storage. Feeds should therefore be treated as pointers. Garrulus should store an abstract only where the licence or Crossref deposit allows it, and give the Elsevier reservation particular weight.

Regional APIs fill specific holes. **SciELO ArticleMeta** is free, keyless and supports incremental harvest by ISSN and date: 111 Zoologia articles since 2025 in one call ([ArticleMeta identifiers](https://articlemeta.scielo.org/api/v1/article/identifiers/?collection=scl&issn=1984-4689&limit=3&from=2025-01-01)). It carries multilingual abstracts, an HTML full-text link and the journal's CC code. That matters because about 40% of Zoologia's recent works lack a Crossref abstract. For Japan, **CiNii Research OpenSearch** (free appid) returns abstracts with an `abstractLicenseFlag` that says whether reuse is allowed ([CiNii OpenSearch](https://support.nii.ac.jp/en/cir/r_opensearch)). Species Diversity, for example, has Crossref DOIs but no abstracts there ([Crossref work](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.12782/specdiv.1.1)). The J-STAGE WebAPI lists articles per ISSN without abstracts. J-STAGE asks for an application for commercial use "regardless of whether the use leads to commercial gain", so an email before use is prudent ([J-STAGE services](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/static/pages/JstageServices/TAB3/-char/en)).

## Some apparent alternatives fail the constraints, and Plazi deserves a role

Several obvious substitutes fail the "free and not OpenAlex" test. **Unpaywall now runs on the OpenAlex codebase** as a "very light transformation" of OpenAlex works, so OA status and licences must come from Crossref licence URLs, DOAJ journal licences and Europe PMC flags instead ([OpenAlex blog](https://blog.openalex.org/category/unpaywall)). **Lens** is effectively paid after a 14-day trial ([SMU API list](https://researchguides.smu.edu.sg/api-list/scholarly-metadata-api)). **BASE** is non-commercial only and IP-whitelisted ([BASE guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/935528536/Base-Interface)). Semantic Scholar is free at 1 request per second, but the licence of the abstracts it serves is unclear ([S2 API licence](https://semanticscholar.org/product/api/license)). The bioRxiv bulk buckets are **requester-pays**, which breaks "free by default" ([AWS Requester Pays](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/dev/RequesterPaysBuckets.html)). Full ISSN metadata starts at €978 a year ([ISSN pricing](https://www.issn.org/services/subscribe-to-the-register/pricing/)). Two regional platforms were unusable: Redalyc's and AmeliCA's OAI endpoints timed out, refused connections or returned login pages, and eLIBRARY.ru has no automated-access terms ([Redalyc OAI page](https://www.redalyc.org/redalyc/acerca-de/oai-pmh.html)). Keyed access is possible for Korea's KCI, which needs an application, and for core Chinese taxonomy journals. The Chinese titles are the hardest case: Acta Entomologica Sinica's DOIs are registered with CNKI and Zoological Systematics' presumably with ISTIC, not Crossref ([doi.org RA lookup](https://doi.org/ra/10.16380,10.11865,10.13057,10.12782,10.25221,10.5281)). Their Beijing Magtech sites offer RSS and English abstracts under "All Rights Reserved" ([insect.org.cn](https://www.insect.org.cn/EN/column/column1.shtml)). That allows a polite, metadata-only adapter at best.

Plazi's **Biodiversity Literature Repository** on Zenodo is not a harvest route for articles, but it is valuable as a signal ([Zenodo biosyslit](https://zenodo.org/communities/biosyslit/browse)). The recovered summary reports that its CC0 treatment deposits point to their article DOI and carry the act in the title ("… comb. nov."). It can be polled through the OAI set `user-biosyslit` or the REST API, subject to Zenodo's search limits tightened in January 2026 ([Zenodo blog](https://blog.zenodo.org/2025/11/25/2025-11-14-search-api-updates/)). That makes it a near-real-time list of DOIs of new taxonomic articles, which can check Garrulus's recall. It is also free ground truth for nomenclatural acts per article. It covers only what Plazi chooses to process, and closed PDFs such as Zootaxa's are deposited as restricted. The nomenclators that are not paid are reference checks rather than feeds. IPNI has an undocumented JSON search with `added`, `published_in` and `issn` terms ([IPNI API](https://www.ipni.org/api/1/search)). ZooBank probes failed. AlgaeBase and Zoological Record are paid.

## A five-step adoption order

The order follows yield per unit of effort, and it keeps every route behind the existing task queue and outage handling.

| Order | Route | What it adds | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crossref REST as primary harvester (both ISSNs, `from-index-date`, 40 ISSNs per call) plus the annual public data file for backfill | Most works, most abstracts, DOIs, licence URLs; replaces the OpenAlex daily harvest and snapshot | Honour per-`mailto` 3 list req/s; test licence URLs for CC terms |
| 2 | Discovery from PKP Beacon, DOAJ (LCC subjects), Plazi srsStats and COL XR references; confirm with Crossref `/journals`; normalise with ISSN-L | Candidate journals, OAI endpoints, journal licences, a discipline facet for OA titles | Rank candidates by names found; re-`Identify` every endpoint |
| 3 | Generic OAI-PMH adapter: ARPHA, OJS via Beacon, Hrčak, DergiPark, AJOL, CyberLeninka, SciELO MX, e-periodica | Non-DOI journals, licences, galley links, JATS/HTML full text | Prefer `jats`, then HTML galleys; OAI id as a third work key; full re-list where `from` fails |
| 4 | Abstract top-up: Europe PMC by ISSN, SN Meta API, SciELO ArticleMeta, CiNii | Abstracts for Elsevier, T&F, SN subscription and Japanese titles | Only for journals with a low Crossref abstract share |
| 5 | Signals: Wiley, Springer and Frontiers RSS; BLR on Zenodo; bioRxiv API | Faster detection, abstracts, act ground truth, preprints | Feeds as pointers; store abstracts only where allowed |

Some routes are deferred until a condition is met: J-STAGE after a permission email, KCI after a key, Redalyc once an endpoint works, and Chinese Magtech sites as a metadata-only crawl. Per-journal HTML adapters for the museum journals without OAI or DOIs come last.

## Conclusion

The real loss from leaving OpenAlex is classification, not harvesting. Crossref plus OAI-PMH reaches more of the taxonomic long tail than OpenAlex's source list did. But no free service now says which journals are about biodiversity, because Crossref dropped subjects, Wikidata is thin and JournalTOCs is gone. Garrulus therefore has to classify journals itself. It already derives each journal's taxonomic focus from its own index, and the same index can rank candidate journals by names found, with Plazi's journal statistics and COL's 2 million references as taxonomy-native priors. Done well, that turns discovery into a feedback loop no generic aggregator offers. Plazi's BLR, an independent stream of new taxonomic DOIs with their acts, then provides the recall check.

The second implication concerns content. The OAI route makes open full text available as HTML from CC-BY OJS journals, in a form that keeps italics, which GROBID's TEI does not. That makes the deferred route 5 less a PDF-and-GROBID project and more an HTML-galley converter. The open questions to settle before building are four: Europe PMC's actual abstract coverage for the Elsevier and T&F biodiversity titles, the Springer Nature Basic monthly cap, the reuse terms of Hrčak, DergiPark, AJOL and CyberLeninka, and whether the Plazi and COL-reference figures, which come from a recovered summary rather than full notes, hold up on a careful re-probe.
